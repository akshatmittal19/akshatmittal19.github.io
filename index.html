COMPLETED MSc THESIS · UCL · SEPTEMBER 2026

Augment, Prioritise, Learn

Data Augmentation and Curriculum Design for Robust Language-Model Reasoning

How can a curriculum keep finding useful challenges as a model improves, and does that learning transfer? My thesis connected ideas from open-ended learning with reinforcement-learning post-training, combining targeted data augmentation and adaptive task selection for mathematical reasoning and long-horizon terminal tasks. Supervised by Dr. Ilija Bogunovic.

  • An extra step exposes brittle reasoning. Adding an intermediate reasoning step was the most disruptive augmentation across 50 mathematical problems and four model–inference configurations.
  • Augment at the learning frontier. Combined targeted subproblem augmentations with open-ended curricula that select challenges near the model’s evolving capability boundary. The gains in robustness depended on how the curriculum used feedback from augmented problems.
  • Better training signals don’t guarantee transfer. In terminal tasks, prioritised replay found more informative training groups but transferred less well than uniform sampling: 34.0% versus 42.5% completion on InterCode-Bash.

What helps a model learn can mislead its curriculum.

Open-ended quadrupedal locomotion

Studied open-ended reinforcement learning through unsupervised environment design: generating and adapting environments to challenge an agent as it learns. Implemented ACCEL/PLR-style curricula in JAX and MuJoCo Playground, with procedural terrain generation, mutation, and replay.
